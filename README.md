# lrfs-online-shoppers-behaviour-based-customer-segmentation
Customer segmentation is a crucial task in e-commerce, helping businesses target the
right audience with personalized strategies. This study explores customer segmentation
for online shoppers using the LRFS (Recency, Frequency, and Monetary, and Spend)
model combined with K-Means clustering and Principal Component Analysis (PCA)
for dimensionality reduction. The LRFS model categorizes customers based on their
purchasing behavior: Recency (R) measures how recently a customer made a purchase,
Frequency (F) quantifies how often a customer makes purchases, Monetary (M) captures
the total spending, and Spend (S) reflects the average transaction value. Given the
complexity and high dimensionality of transactional data, PCA is applied to reduce the
feature space, preserving the most critical components while minimizing noise. K-Means
clustering is then used to segment customers into distinct groups with similar purchasing
patterns. The combined approach allows for efficient segmentation, leading to the
identification of high-value customer segments, as well as potential opportunities for
targeted marketing and resource allocation. The results demonstrate that integrating LRFS
metrics with PCA enhances the performance and interpretability of the K-Means
clustering process, offering actionable insights for e-commerce businesses seeking to
optimize customer engagement.
In the rapidly evolving world of e-commerce, understanding customer behavior and
preferences is crucial for businesses to remain competitive and enhance their
market strategies. One effective approach for gaining insights into customer
segments is through customer segmentation. This process allows businesses to
categorize customers into distinct groups based on similar behaviors or attributes,
enabling personalized marketing, targeted promotions, and improved customer
service.
The use of advanced machine learning techniques such as K-means clustering and
Principal Component Analysis (PCA) has proven highly effective in segmenting
customers based on vast amounts of online shopping data. K-means clustering is
an unsupervised algorithm that groups data into clusters based on similarities,
while PCA is a dimensionality reduction technique that simplifies the complexity of
data by transforming it into fewer variables (principal components), retaining the
most significant information.
In this context, the application of K-means clustering, combined with PCA, enables
businesses to identify hidden patterns in customer behavior, such as purchasing
frequency, product preferences, and browsing habits, all while reducing data
complexity. This approach helps businesses craft highly targeted marketing
strategies, optimize product offerings, and enhance customer satisfaction. The
implementation of these algorithms can lead to increased sales, higher customer
retention rates, and more efficient resource allocation.
This study explores how K-means clustering and PCA can be used to segment online
shoppers, providing valuable insights for e-commerce businesses aiming to
understand their customers better and drive growth in a competitive online
marketplace.
